Project teaser

A frozen Motion Diffusion Model is refined at sampling time with phase-aware geometric constraints, from local ground contact to multi-stair interaction and long-horizon composition.

Abstract

Human motion diffusion models can generate diverse and visually plausible motions, but their outputs do not inherently satisfy terrain-dependent geometric and contact requirements. This can leave ground penetration, floating feet, support drift, and lower-leg–terrain intersections even when the overall motion appears natural.

We introduce a unified sampling-time constraint framework for a pretrained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM). Differentiable geometric and phase-dependent constraints are evaluated on the predicted clean motion during reverse diffusion and incorporated through an augmented Lagrangian method (ALM), while the denoising model remains frozen. The framework progresses from generic ground-contact correction to phase-aware platform interaction, fixed-dimensional continuous multi-stair refinement, and long-horizon motion composition through temporal inpainting and consistent motion–terrain registration.

Qualitative results

Generic Ground-Contact Correction

Matched baseline-versus-ALM examples across different text-generated actions. The pooled generated-contact floating rate decreases from 31.36% to 3.10%, and all measured floor-penetration cases in the evaluated configurations are removed.

Orange markers indicate foot floating during generated contact, green markers indicate valid ground contact, red markers indicate ground penetration, and blue markers indicate swing. Phase-aware terrain refinement

Platform Interaction

The same sampling-time framework activates clearance, landing, settled-contact, no-slip, release, and lower-leg–riser constraints according to the detected interaction phase.

Fixed-dimensional extension

Continuous Multi-Stair Refinement

Repeated stair edges are handled without allocating a new ALM residual block for every edge. In the evaluated 120-frame sequence with three successive risers, two of five stochastic refinements remove all 14 reference terrain-penetration frames; the remaining samples retain only sparse millimeter-scale penetration.

Two-sided temporal inpainting, spatial alignment, motion–terrain registration, and transition-local refinement extend corrected motion beyond MDM's 196-frame native window. The three evaluated routes contain 385–430 frames.

Takeaway

Within the evaluated kinematic setting, terrain-dependent geometric constraints can be introduced directly during sampling of a frozen human motion diffusion model. The framework substantially reduces the targeted contact and terrain-interaction artifacts while largely retaining the main motion structure and visual characteristics. The method remains a soft kinematic constraint approach and does not provide a hard guarantee of dynamic executability.
